## C1 / mechanism and security: wounded (revised 8.2)
FATAL:
  - Nothing in the core (items 1–6) is fatal. Every core primitive was verified permissionless and working, including an eth_call simulation of the one-transaction borrow. One module is fatal as written: the BufferedLendingStrategy at the climax of the wow moment. sMUSD implementation 0xb33c…29e87 makes deposit and withdraw nonReentrant and calls strategy.allocate and strategy.deallocate inside that lock. Supplying the strategy into vMUSD, whose liquidity adapter is wsMUSD, which calls sMUSD.deposit, therefore re-enters sMUSD and reverts every sMUSD deposit and withdraw. Supplying M1 directly instead still breaks IVaultStrategy's rule that deallocate 'must deallocate exactly' the principal: any M1 bad debt would strand the last ≈25% of the 7.38M sMUSD. Mezo's 4-of-7 multisig cannot responsibly call setStrategy on this, and demoing it as the climax invites an over-claiming score.
SERIOUS:
  - Liquidation feasibility is far worse than claimed, and depth swings. Today CL200 holds 9,956 MUSD, not 15.7k. QuoterV2 shows 0.2 BTC exhausting the pool at 9,901 MUSD; the candidate claims 15,982. Clean depth within the 4.38% liquidation incentive is ≈0.10–0.12 BTC (≈$8–10k). The vAMM loses 8% on 0.05 BTC and 28.5% on 0.2 BTC. CL200's MUSD side went 22.3k → 15.4k → 16.0k → 9.96k in 43 h, and in one hour a 0.067 BTC inflow drained 34% while Skip was flat. In a crash, arbitrage likely drains it before liquidators arrive [I]. Mainnet Morpho has 0 Liquidate events ever, so there is no external liquidator ecosystem and the team's keeper or backstop is a single point of failure. The 25k M1 cap and 0.2 BTC per account rest on depth that does not exist, so the backstop is core, not a module.
  - Position limits are front-end and clone rules only: ≤0.2 BTC per account, opening LTV ≤71%, LLTV − 15 pts. Anyone can borrow from M1 directly at up to 86% LTV and any size. Vault V2 caps bound vMUSD's total exposure but not the size of one position, and cutting caps or deallocating never shrinks existing borrows.
  - The stated parameters create a deposit DoS. Vault V2 enter() calls allocateInternal(liquidityAdapter) without try/catch, and its cap checks revert (verified in vault-v2 source). wsMUSD's 50k cap is below vMUSD's 100k cap and is shared with bMUSD and direct holders. Once wsMUSD is full (anyone can fill it), or if it is paused, every vMUSD deposit reverts. A vault cap on the wsMUSD adapter id below the vault size does the same.
  - M2 (wsMUSD collateral, mUSDC loan) is structurally under-liquid. The whole MUSD→mUSDC exit is CL10 ≈9.27k mUSDC (1k → 989.1; 10k and 20k → 9,269.4, exhausted) plus the sAMM's 4.83k mUSDC, against 122k mUSDC supply. Above ≈$5k, M2 is a free put on MUSD paid for by mUSDC lenders, and Enclave A's veBTC #6114 unlock on 2026-11-12 is a plausible depeg trigger [I]. WsMusdOracle's behaviour on stale Pyth is unspecified. decimalsOffset makes wsMUSD 18+offset decimals, so convertToAssets(1e18) mis-scales.
  - The backstop (bMUSD) has the wrong economics on Morpho. Liquidations have no priority, so external flash liquidators using CL10's 5.33M MUSD at a 0.05% flash fee take the profitable ones. The backstop is left with crash-time and oversize positions, held as BTC with a slow exit [I]. Its NAV jumps at each liquidation, so a just-in-time flash deposit can capture the premium, and depositors can exit ahead of a loss. The BAMM 'port' is mostly a rewrite (its Stability Pool plumbing does not apply), which adds audit surface.
  - EscapeHatch: closeTroveWithSignature has no caller restriction and burns msg.sender's MUSD (verified in source). Anyone who sees a signature can execute it and route the BTC to the signed recipient. If that recipient is a shared EscapeHatch that accounts by balance, an attacker pays debt D and captures the ≈1.4D of stranded BTC as the 'next user'. Its mainnet market is also zero: there is one trove and it cannot close.
  - Vault V2 charges its performance fee on all interest, including the wsMUSD savings yield. When M1 is idle, vMUSD lenders earn 90% of the sMUSD rate, so 'lenders lose nothing versus sMUSD' is false. Vault V2 also accrues nothing until maxRate is set.
  - sMUSD is a single point of failure. An immutable wsMUSD wraps a proxy that the 4-of-7 multisig can upgrade with no timelock (5 Upgraded events so far), and the proxy still trusts MezoForwarder (isTrustedForwarder = true). One semantic upgrade would hit wsMUSD, vMUSD's liquidity floor, bMUSD's idle cash and M2's collateral at once. No in-kind exit is specified.
  - Oracle liveness: if Skip is older than 60 s, or Skip and Pyth diverge beyond the ±10% band, price() reverts. That freezes M1 borrows, collateral withdrawals with debt outstanding, and liquidations. Pyth is pushed hourly, so the band is live almost all the time. The worst-case freeze is ≈65 min, until Pyth ages past 3,900 s. This is acceptable, and the MUSD CDP has the same Skip exposure, but it must be disclosed.
  - Testnet claims and funding. 'First BTC→MUSD market' is false on testnet: Mezo-owned Morpho 0x6F1a… already has a BTC→MUSD market at LLTV 0.86 and holds 99M test MUSD. The demo needs 20–50k test MUSD plus 0.03–0.04 test BTC per trove kit, while one faucet drip buys ≈78.65 MUSD, so the demo depends on DevRel. QuoterV2 is absent on testnet, so depth-aware caps cannot run there as written.
  - Viability and over-claiming. Mezo's only Morpho market has had 16 distinct borrowers ever, so M1 demand during judging will likely be only the team's seed [I]. Fees are ≈$875/yr per 25k lent at 3.5%, so the fee loop is cosmetic. The wow-moment chart (sMUSD's yield source switching, voter income lasting past the December cliff) depends on governance and on demand that does not exist. In the come-back flow, 67.6% of the 5.70 BTC sits with four owners who have been dormant for 6–12 months, and 0.864 BTC sits in Safe proxies that cannot sign ECDSA. There is no channel to reach either group.
FIXES:
  - Size the mainnet launch to measured depth. Set M1's absolute cap to min(2 × funded backstop MUSD, backstop MUSD + the 24 h-minimum depth at 4% slippage), and keep it ≤10k MUSD at launch. Cap each position at ≤0.05 BTC. Make the backstop, or keeper inventory, part of the core. Publish the depth snapshot.
  - Make position limits real. Either use a BTC collateral wrapper that only BorrowAccount clones can mint and anyone can unwrap, or size caps to a single whale borrowing at 86% LTV. Say plainly that cutting caps does not reduce existing exposure.
  - Fix the vMUSD deposit DoS. Remove wsMUSD's 50k cap, or keep it far above the sum of the caps that depend on it. Never pause wsMUSD deposits. Set the vault's caps on the wsMUSD adapter id at or above the vault cap. Alternatively, use an adapter that deposits min(assets, maxDeposit) and leaves the rest idle. Set maxRate at deployment.
  - Launch vMUSD with a 0 performance fee, or use an adapter that takes a fee only on M1 interest. Drop the claim that lenders lose nothing versus sMUSD.
  - Drop M2 from mainnet and keep it as a testnet demo capped at ≤5k. Make WsMusdOracle revert when Pyth MUSD is stale and scale by 10**decimals(). Re-check M2 around the 2026-11-12 Enclave unlock.
  - Rebuild the backstop natively for Morpho. Add a deposit lock or cooldown and vest liquidation premiums linearly. Disclose the BTC inventory risk. Make the primary liquidation path a permissionless flash liquidator: borrow MUSD via CL10 flash, liquidate, then sell on CL200 within a price-impact bound.
  - Make every signed CloseTrove or ClaimCollateral pay the user's own CREATE2 clone. Account by balance changes within the call and never sweep balances. Move EscapeHatch to the fork-test demo.
  - Remove setStrategy from the wow moment. If the strategy is kept as a proposal, it should supply M1 directly and never re-enter sMUSD. It should carry first-loss cover at least equal to its exposure, be capped at or below M1's cap, and be fork-tested against the real sMUSD implementation 0xb33c….
  - Add an in-kind exit: wsMUSD redemption that returns sMUSD. Have the sentinel cut caps automatically on any sMUSD upgrade or strategy change.
  - Keep Skip's 60 s bound and the ±10% Pyth band. Disclose the worst-case freeze of about 65 minutes. Show the oracle-free exit in the UI: repay in full, then withdraw collateral.
  - Claim 'first on Mezo mainnet', not first overall. Update the depth figures to 9,956 MUSD of CL200 depth and a 0.2 BTC sale exhausting the pool at 9,901 MUSD. State the 16-borrower baseline. Label the yield-cliff chart as a scenario and give its fees in dollars.
  - Ask DevRel for test BTC and test MUSD now. Budget the fallback at ≈78.65 MUSD per faucet drip. Cut the trove kits unless funded. Add a testnet quoter. Get a real openAndBorrow msg.value transaction onto the evidence page early.

## C1 / market and judging: would Mezo's judges really rank it #1? (overlap, pipeline, Mezo roadmap, business-case realism, demo risk, faucet testability, over-claiming): wounded (revised 7.8)
FATAL:
  - Fatal to the strategy module as written, and to credibility if it is demoed as a one-call drop-in. In the sMUSD implementation (0xb33c…9e87), deposit() and withdraw() are nonReentrant and call strategy.allocate() and strategy.deallocate() while the lock is held. receiveYieldFromStrategy() calls strategy.claimYield() with no try/catch. So BufferedLendingStrategy → vMUSD.deposit → wsMUSD liquidity adapter → sMUSD.deposit re-enters the lock and reverts. The path is also circular, because sMUSD principal would be backing sMUSD. Separately, any claimYield revert, such as withdrawing interest from M1 at 100% utilisation, would brick every sMUSD deposit and withdraw. Mezo's MUSD engineers would catch this on first read.
  - Fatal to the wow moment and the core viability story as pitched (over-claiming, which judges penalise). The cliff itself is real: 12,207 of the 19,367 MUSD that sMUSD received over the last 30 days (63.0%) came through YieldConverter, so after the cliff inflow falls to about 7,161 MUSD per 30 days, roughly 1.2% APR. The gauge's ~72% share of that is about 1.2k MUSD a week, against the 10k a week voters are paid now. Keeping voter income at 10k a week would need about $18–20M of M1 borrowing at ≈3.5%. Mainnet liquidation depth supports an M1 cap of about $10–25k, so the shortfall is roughly 200–800×. The chart showing 'veBTC voters' MUSD income extending past the December cliff' would in fact be a flat line, and it contradicts the candidate's own 'honest scale' section.
SERIOUS:
  - Demand is unproven, and the causes of the trove collapse are untouched. M1 borrowers still have to sell MUSD at ≈0.989, and the mUSDC bid on Mezo is only about $14k: CL10 holds 9,277 mUSDC, so selling 10k MUSD returns 9,269, and the sAMM holds 4,834 mUSDC. Mezo's own BTC/mUSDC market, a better product, is limited by demand, not by supply. It has 81,860.7 supplied and 66,328.5 borrowed (81.0% utilisation, 15.5k mUSDC idle), and AdaptiveCurveIrm's rateAtTarget has decayed from 4% to 3.54%. That refutes go-to-market #2 ('BTC/mUSDC borrowers capped by the 122k supply').
  - Liquidation depth is worse than the brief says. CL200 now holds 9,956 MUSD (the brief had 15.7k). QuoterV2 gives 0.1 BTC → 8,081.8 MUSD (clean) but 0.2 BTC → 9,900.9, 39% under Skip, because the pool runs out. The vAMM gives 0.2 BTC → 11,568.6 (−28.5%). The '0.2-BTC clean-liquidation depth' cap formula and the ≤0.2 BTC per-account limit are stale. Clean depth is about 0.1 BTC (≈$8k), so M1 on mainnet is toy-sized.
  - There is prior art that Mezo's judges will know. A Mezo-adjacent EOA, 0x44EC…c38B, which also administers the tokenized-stock tokens, deployed a second Morpho Blue at 0x5e48…cAbf with LLTV 0.77 enabled. It holds 6 MUSD-loan markets against BTDR, COIN, DEFT, FLD, GLXY and MSTR (created 2025-11-09) and 'Mezo Morpho X Vault' MetaMorpho vaults. Those vaults held about 1,000 MUSD each, saw 47 borrows of 0.5–45 MUSD and no liquidations, and were redeemed on 2026-06-26. So MUSD lending on Morpho is not new to Mezo. M1 plus vMUSD is a configuration that Mezo's deployer, which built the USDC stack, could copy. The novelty rests on BTC collateral on the official Morpho, wsMUSD, and the guarded oracle.
  - The 'first' claims overlap with Mezodot (submitted) and Cosign (pipeline). Mezodot already lends MUSD against BTC (LTV 70%, liquidation threshold 78%), has ERC-4626 pMUSD collateral, ERC-3156 MUSD flash loans at 0.09%, and a one-transaction BTC long, all bespoke and on testnet. Cosign (pushed 2026-10-23) runs MUSD-loan Morpho markets with a tranche vault, liquidator and gauge, 40 tests and 35 recorded transactions. It does so on its own testnet Morpho with MEZO collateral and an AdminOracle. The claims must be scoped to 'first BTC→MUSD market on Mezo's official Morpho, on mainnet'.
  - The go-to-market pools are overstated. Unstaked sMUSD is about 2.07M, and 2.0M of that sits in one Safe (0xf365…8BC4), so retail unstaked sMUSD is about $70k. Enclave A is institutional. CollSurplusPool does have 27 accounts, but the top four hold 3.855 BTC (67.6%). They were redeemed out about four days ago, and each can self-claim with one call, so the come-back pool will shrink before Wave 2.
  - Router-only limits are presented as risk parameters. 'Opening LTV ≤71%', 'per-account ≤0.2 BTC' and 'pause new borrows' bind only BorrowAccount. Anyone can borrow directly on M1 up to 0.86. The only market-level controls are Vault V2 caps, deallocation and the LLTV, and engineers who read the code will notice.
  - The allocator rule starves its own market. Moving MUSD into M1 only while M1's supply APY beats wsMUSD leaves nothing to borrow at launch, because supply APY is 0 when nothing is borrowed. The market needs a standing idle buffer.
  - 'Lenders lose nothing versus sMUSD' is false. vMUSD lenders bear socialised bad debt from a market that cannot be paused and liquidates into about $8k of clean depth. They also pay a 10% performance fee and carry wrapper and adapter risk.
  - Funding the testnet demo is a real risk. 0.001 test BTC buys 78.65 test MUSD, so the planned 20–50k test MUSD costs about 0.25–0.64 test BTC, or 250–640 faucet drips. Test mUSDC has no public mint. Each EscapeHatch kit needs 0.03–0.04 test BTC per judge. Canary liquidations depend on real BTC moves. All of this rests on an unconfirmed DevRel grant [U].
  - Many features cannot be tested by a judge. The come-back flow is mainnet-fork only, the strategy runs on the team's own MUSDSavingsRate instance, EscapeHatch needs sponsored kits, backstop demos depend on canary timing, the fee loop needs fees that do not exist, and Phase 3 is unbuilt. That fails the judges' stated bar ('users can fully test all features'), and an XL scope in a 14-day build invites stubs.
  - EscapeHatch and M2 hurt more than they help. EscapeHatch has no mainnet market (one trove, which cannot close) and literally moves users off Mezo's CDP, which reads as a detour. M2 lends scarce USDC (122k total supply) against MUSD, whose redemption floor is effectively gone, and its liquidations would sell into about $14k of bid. Mezo's curator allocating to it (ask c) is improbable.
  - The core gives MEZO no utility, although Track 1 asks about MUSD and MEZO. The framing also reads as competition with Mezo's own products: 'Redemption risk: none' and 'Mezo's oracle only checks answer > 0'. The second is accurate (MorphoPriceOracle reverts only when answer ≤ 0 and never checks updatedAt), but a core-team panel may not welcome it.
  - Judging-lens conclusion [inference]: Baserate can plausibly finish #1, but it is not secure. With fixes I estimate ≈7.8, against roughly 7.4–7.6 each for Pylon, mbLP, Mezodot and Cosign if Cosign submits. AKINDO point ties are common. The persona judges' viability scores of 8–9 are too generous given the verified demand and depth data.
